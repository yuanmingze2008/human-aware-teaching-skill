# Scientific grounding

The README builds one argument: teaching decisions should connect the learner's current understanding to the cognitive processes that instruction can support. These ten sources come from the literature curated during product development and motivate the skill's design.

## Teaching depends on the learner and the task

**National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine. (2018).** *How People Learn II: Learners, Contexts, and Cultures*. National Academies Press. DOI: 10.17226/24783.

[Chapter 4: Processes That Support Learning](https://uwnxt.nationalacademies.org/read/24783/chapter/6) describes the coordination of attention, memory, metacognition, and self-regulation. [Chapter 5: Knowledge and Reasoning](https://uwnxt.nationalacademies.org/read/24783/chapter/7), especially Conclusions 5-1 through 5-4, pp. 106–107, connects learning strategies to prior knowledge and goals. [Chapter 6: Motivation to Learn](https://uwnxt.nationalacademies.org/read/24783/chapter/8) develops the role of motivation and context.

Prior knowledge shapes how learners interpret and organize new information. Strategy effectiveness depends on existing knowledge and skills, the material, and the goal. This supports the README's starting point: instruction acts on a learner with particular resources and constraints.

**Kalyuga, S., Ayres, P., Chandler, P., & Sweller, J. (2003).** The expertise reversal effect. *Educational Psychologist, 38*(1), 23–31. [DOI: 10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4](https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_4).

The review describes how instructional guidance interacts with limited working memory and stored schemas. Support that helps inexperienced learners can become redundant or interfere with learning as expertise grows. It supplies a concrete example of why the appropriate level of guidance changes with the learner's knowledge.

**Product implication:** choose the amount and form of support from what the learner can currently use, and revise that choice as they progress.

## Activities act through cognitive processes

**Koedinger, K. R., Corbett, A. T., & Perfetti, C. (2012).** The Knowledge-Learning-Instruction Framework: Bridging the Science-Practice Chasm to Enhance Robust Student Learning. *Cognitive Science, 36*, 757–798. DOI: 10.1111/j.1551-6709.2012.01245.x.

[Original paper](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/ED535880.pdf), especially the abstract and introductory account of the framework, pp. 757–760.

KLI coordinates taxonomies of knowledge, learning processes, and instructional conditions. Different processes produce different knowledge changes and constrain effective instruction. This provides a useful level of explanation between a broad teaching method and the learning it is meant to produce.

**Reinhold, F., et al. (2024).** Learning Mechanisms Explaining Learning With Digital Tools in Educational Settings: a Cognitive Process Framework. *Educational Psychology Review*. DOI: 10.1007/s10648-024-09845-6.

[Original article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-024-09845-6), especially the CoDiL framework, Fig. 1, and its discussion of learning activities and mechanisms.

CoDiL distinguishes external learning opportunities, observable learner activity, internal learner characteristics and cognitive processes, and learning outcomes. An activity's observable form leaves open which processes it induces. This supports the README's distinction between providing an example and understanding what the learner does with it.

**Product implication:** connect a proposed teaching move to the change in understanding it is intended to support. Interpret the learner's next response as evidence for revising that decision.

## Similar difficulties can call for different support

**Karpicke, J. D., & Blunt, J. R. (2011).** Retrieval practice produces more learning than elaborative studying with concept mapping. *Science, 331*, 772–775. DOI: 10.1126/science.1199327.

[Original paper at the author's Purdue laboratory](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2011/2011_Karpicke_Blunt_Science.pdf).

In the reported science-text experiments, retrieval practice produced greater learning than elaborative concept mapping, including on comprehension and inference questions. Retrieving knowledge can contribute to learning as well as reveal what is accessible.

**Gick, M. L., & Holyoak, K. J. (1983).** Schema induction and analogical transfer. *Cognitive Psychology, 15*(1), 1–38. DOI: 10.1016/0010-0285(83)90002-6.

[Original paper at the author's UCLA laboratory](https://reasoninglab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/273/2021/04/Gick_Holyoak1983_SchemaInduction.pdf), especially Part II.

Comparing two analogs supported schema induction, and the quality of the resulting schema predicted subsequent transfer. The study connects transfer to the representation of relations shared across examples.

**Product implication:** when a changed problem causes difficulty, consider both access to relevant knowledge and the learner's representation of the shared structure. The cue-versus-comparison scenario in the README illustrates this design reasoning.

## Personalization follows evidence as it changes

**Pashler, H., McDaniel, M., Rohrer, D., & Bjork, R. (2008/2009).** Learning styles: Concepts and evidence. *Psychological Science in the Public Interest, 9*(3), 105–119. DOI: 10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x.

[Original journal paper, university-hosted PDF](https://people.uncw.edu/kozloffm/nolearningstylespdf.pdf), especially the summary and conclusions. The volume is dated 2008; the article was published in 2009.

The review distinguishes expressed preferences and individual aptitudes from evidence that matching instruction to a diagnosed learning style improves learning. It found inadequate support for applying learning-style assessments in general educational practice.

**Product implication:** respect explicit preferences and adapt to demonstrated knowledge, current goals, and task-specific difficulties. Keep broader assumptions revisable as new evidence arrives.

## AI tutoring outcomes depend on interaction design

**Jurenka, I., et al. (2024).** Towards Responsible Development of Generative AI for Education: An Evaluation-Driven Approach. arXiv:2407.12687.

[Original report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.12687).

The LearnLM report describes translating learning-science principles into evaluations and fine-tuning data for educational behavior. It establishes relevant context: pedagogical behavior is already an explicit design target in generative AI. Human-Aware Teaching's product emphasis is the reasoning that connects learner evidence to the next response.

**Bastani, H., et al. (2025).** Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 122*(26), e2422633122. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.2422633122.

[Original article](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122), especially the abstract, intervention design, and unassisted-exam results.

The field experiment compared a GPT-4 assistant, a tutor with learning guardrails, and a control condition. The basic assistant raised assisted practice performance by 48% relative to control; its group subsequently scored 17% lower on unassisted exams. Guardrails largely removed that penalty. These percentages describe relative differences, not percentage-point changes.

**Kestin, G., Miller, K., Klales, A., Milbourne, T., & Ponti, G. (2025).** AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning: an RCT introducing a novel research-based design in an authentic educational setting. *Scientific Reports, 15*, 17458. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6.

[Original article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-97652-6) · [Journal PDF, online mirror](https://atelierdesfuturs.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/AI-tutoring-outperforms-in-class-active-learning.pdf).

In a randomized crossover study in undergraduate physics, a tutor designed around pedagogical best practices produced higher post-test performance than in-class active learning. It supplies a complementary example of effective AI tutoring design.

**Product implication:** design the interaction around the learner's goal and developing capability. For goals that require independent use, let evidence of independent performance guide support and its gradual removal.
