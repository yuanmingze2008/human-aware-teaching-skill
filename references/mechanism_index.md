# Mechanism Index

Use this file to decide where to look. Start from learner evidence, not from mechanism names.

## Fast routing by observed difficulty

### "There is too much going on" / learner watches the wrong thing
Read `mechanisms/attention_processing.md`.
Typical questions:
- Is attention on the diagnostic feature?
- Must too many unfamiliar elements be held or integrated at once?

### "I know the pieces, but I do not understand it"
Read `mechanisms/representation_coherence.md`.
Typical questions:
- Is prerequisite knowledge absent, inactive, or misleading?
- Is a relation or inference missing?
- Does the learner lack a usable representation or translation between representations?
- Is there a causal/generative model, or only facts and procedures?

### Learner is mainly watching, copying, or being heavily guided
Read `mechanisms/construction_guidance.md`.
Typical questions:
- Would generation expose or build missing relations?
- Is demonstration useful now?
- Is the learner reproducing a procedure without principle-level understanding?
- Has support become unnecessary?

### Correction did not "stick" / the same wrong model returns
Read `mechanisms/feedback_revision.md`.
Typical questions:
- Did the learner interpret the feedback?
- Did contradictory evidence actually change the old model?
- Are old and new models competing across contexts?

### "I knew this before" / cueing suddenly makes it available
Read `mechanisms/memory_accessibility.md`.
Typical questions:
- Is the problem retrieval or applicability rather than understanding?
- Is repeated exposure being confused with retrieval?
- Is long-term retention the actual goal?
- Is lack of fluency consuming resources needed for higher reasoning?

### Original example works, changed example fails
Read `mechanisms/abstraction_transfer.md`, and often cross-check `memory_accessibility.md`.
Typical questions:
- Has the learner aligned relational structure across examples?
- Have variation and contrasts revealed invariants and boundaries?
- Is knowledge bound to surface form or context?
- If a small cue restores performance, the main issue may be retrieval/applicability rather than representation.

### "I understand" may not match performance / learner does not know when to change strategy
Read `mechanisms/metacognition.md`.
Typical questions:
- Is confidence being driven by familiarity, fluency, or immediate success?
- Can the learner monitor difficulty and select a more useful strategy?

## Selection rule

Enter the smallest plausible family set. Read only mechanisms that could change the response.

When two mechanisms remain plausible, do not force a diagnosis. First ask whether the distinction would change the response.

- If the same move is sensible under both, use it and observe what happens.
- If the mechanisms imply different moves, seek the smallest discriminating evidence. Prefer evidence whose plausible outcomes clearly separate the alternatives and therefore change what you would do next.

Diagnostic interaction is valuable for decision-making, not for completing a taxonomy.
