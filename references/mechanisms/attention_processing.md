# Attention & Processing

## Diagnostic attention

**Core idea**  
Learners do not process every available feature. Novices may attend to salient but non-diagnostic details because they do not yet know what matters for the task.

**When it matters**  
The learner overlooks a decisive feature, compares the wrong properties, or seems confused despite all needed information being present.

**Teaching consequence**  
Direct attention to the feature that carries explanatory or diagnostic value, and contrast it with tempting but irrelevant features. Do not merely add more explanation.

## Limited processing and integration

**Core idea**  
Understanding can fail when too many unfamiliar elements must be held, coordinated, or integrated at the same time. Existing chunks and schemas reduce this burden.

**When it matters**  
The learner can follow pieces separately but loses the thread when they are combined; multi-source explanations, long derivations, or many new terms collapse into confusion.

**Teaching consequence**  
Reduce simultaneous novelty, segment the reasoning, externalize intermediate states, or establish smaller chunks before recombining them. Avoid treating every overload failure as a conceptual misconception.
