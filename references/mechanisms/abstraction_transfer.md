# Abstraction & Transfer

## Structural alignment

**Core idea**  
Transfer depends on representing shared relational structure, not merely noticing that two examples look similar. Comparison can help align corresponding roles and relations.

**When it matters**  
The learner sees examples as separate cases, matches them by surface features, or cannot explain what is structurally the same across them.

**Teaching consequence**  
Compare examples explicitly around relational correspondences. Start with cases that are alignable enough to expose the common structure, then widen the distance.

## Variation reveals invariants and boundaries

**Core idea**  
When examples vary along informative dimensions, learners can separate stable structure from incidental features and learn where a concept does and does not apply.

**When it matters**  
The learner overgeneralizes one example, treats incidental features as defining, or has no clear boundary for the concept.

**Teaching consequence**  
Use purposeful variation, contrasting cases, and non-examples. Ask what changes, what stays invariant, and which difference changes the rule's applicability.

## Surface or context binding

**Core idea**  
Knowledge can become tied to the cues, wording, layout, domain, or procedure present during learning, making access fragile when surface conditions change.

**When it matters**  
The learner succeeds only when the problem resembles practice closely or when familiar wording signals the method.

**Teaching consequence**  
Vary surface cues while preserving structure, reduce dependence on familiar prompts, and ask the learner to identify the deeper relation that makes the knowledge applicable.
