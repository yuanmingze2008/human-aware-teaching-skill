# Representation & Coherence

## Prerequisite or prior-model problem

**Core idea**  
New information is interpreted through what the learner already knows. Required prior knowledge may be absent, inactive, partial, or actively misleading.

**When it matters**  
A new concept repeatedly fails to connect, the learner uses an old rule inappropriately, or a seemingly simple explanation presupposes knowledge they cannot actually use.

**Teaching consequence**  
Identify the smallest prerequisite or prior model that changes interpretation. Activate, repair, or replace that structure instead of repeatedly restating the new material.

## Missing global organizing schema

**Core idea**  
In a large unfamiliar knowledge space, local facts are hard to place when the learner lacks a coarse map of parts, boundaries, and relations.

**When it matters**  
The learner can follow individual details but cannot say where they fit, what belongs together, or how the topic is organized.

**Teaching consequence**  
Provide a low-resolution structure first: major components, their roles, and how the current detail fits. Keep the map simpler than the content it organizes.

## Missing inferential bridge

**Core idea**  
A learner may understand both endpoints of an explanation while lacking the relation, assumption, or intermediate inference that connects them.

**When it matters**  
They say a transition feels arbitrary, can repeat A and C but cannot explain why A leads to C, or become unstuck when one hidden step is supplied.

**Teaching consequence**  
Repair the specific bridge. Expose the hidden assumption, add one intermediate step, or ask the learner to reconstruct the transition. Do not restart the whole explanation by default.

## No usable representation for an abstraction

**Core idea**  
An abstract relation can remain verbal and inert when the learner has no manipulable or interpretable internal representation for it.

**When it matters**  
Definitions are repeated accurately but remain empty; the learner cannot imagine, predict, instantiate, or operate with the idea.

**Teaching consequence**  
Create a foothold using a concrete case, spatial/visual structure, executable example, or tightly mapped analogy, then connect it explicitly back to the abstraction and its limits.

## Representation translation gap

**Core idea**  
Knowing one representation does not guarantee understanding the correspondence among words, symbols, diagrams, code, graphs, or physical actions.

**When it matters**  
The learner succeeds in one format but fails when the same structure is expressed differently, or cannot explain what elements in one representation correspond to in another.

**Teaching consequence**  
Make the mapping itself the learning target. Translate in both directions and ask what is preserved, changed, or emphasized by each representation.

## Incomplete causal or generative model

**Core idea**  
Deep understanding often includes a model that can generate predictions about why something happens and what changes when conditions change, not just a description or procedure.

**When it matters**  
The learner knows what happens or how to execute steps but cannot predict interventions, diagnose failures, explain mechanisms, or reason counterfactually.

**Teaching consequence**  
Build or test the mechanism: ask for predictions, vary one condition, compare competing causal explanations, or trace how a change propagates through the system.
