# Metacognition

## Metacognitive judgment uses imperfect cues

**Core idea**  
Learners estimate understanding using cues such as familiarity, processing fluency, recent success, confidence, and the ease of following an explanation. These cues can diverge from later independent performance.

**When it matters**  
The learner says "I get it" after a fluent explanation, overestimates mastery after supported success, or feels lost merely because active learning is effortful.

**Teaching consequence**  
Calibrate confidence with lightweight evidence appropriate to the goal: independent recall, explanation, prediction, or application. Do not turn every interaction into formal testing.

## Monitoring and strategy control can fail or develop

**Core idea**  
Learners must notice difficulty, identify its source, and choose useful strategies; knowing a strategy exists does not mean knowing when or how to deploy it.

**When it matters**  
The learner persists with an unhelpful approach, cannot tell what kind of help is needed, or improves when prompted to externalize, compare, retrieve, or change representation.

**Teaching consequence**  
Make the strategic decision visible and gradually hand it back to the learner: what signal indicated a problem, what strategy fits that signal, and when to stop using it.
