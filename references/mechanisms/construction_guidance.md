# Construction & Guidance

## Generative processing versus passive reception

**Core idea**  
When learners predict, explain, derive, compare, organize, or choose, they must construct relations themselves rather than only receive an already organized structure.

**When it matters**  
The learner appears to follow an explanation but gives little evidence of an internally usable structure, or a small generation task could reveal what is missing.

**Teaching consequence**  
Use a targeted act of generation when it has diagnostic or constructive value. Do not turn every explanation into a quiz; generation should serve a specific cognitive purpose.

## Learning from models and worked examples

**Core idea**  
A comprehensible model can provide an initial process structure and reduce unproductive search, especially when the learner cannot yet generate the process alone.

**When it matters**  
The learner lacks a starting procedure, repeatedly searches blindly, or would benefit from seeing hidden reasoning made visible.

**Teaching consequence**  
Model the relevant process clearly, expose key decision points, and then shift attention from copying the sequence to understanding why the moves work.

## Procedural reproduction without principle

**Core idea**  
A learner can reproduce a demonstrated sequence without representing how each step serves a goal or principle. Surface changes then break performance.

**When it matters**  
They can repeat the worked example but cannot justify steps, adapt them, or solve a structurally similar problem with different surface features.

**Teaching consequence**  
Connect step -> subgoal -> principle, ask for rationale or prediction at selected points, and test a changed case before adding more identical examples.

## Guidance usefulness changes with learner state

**Core idea**  
The same hint, explanation, example, or scaffold can be enabling at one knowledge state and redundant or constraining at another.

**When it matters**  
The learner has begun succeeding independently, waits for help they no longer need, or shows friction with explanations that were previously useful.

**Teaching consequence**  
Fade support based on demonstrated independence. Conversely, restore support when a task introduces genuinely new structure rather than assuming progress is globally transferable.
